The National Innovation Challenge for Drone Application and Research (NIDAR), is currently India's largest drone innovation challenge, organised by the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY) together with the Drone Federation of India (DFI). 2026 marked the very first edition of the competition, drawing 351 teams from across the country attempting to solve two key problem statements: precision agriculture and disaster management.

For Project MANAS, this marked the first time we attempted a fully coordinated multi-drone autonomous system. We tackled the precision agriculture problem statement which required an autonomous multi-drone system to identify yellow patches on an agricultural field and spray it with a 1 foot accuracy.

The Intelligent Ground Vehicle Competition (IGVC) estd. in 1993 by Oakland University has grown into one of the biggest international events for autonomous vehicles. This year's edition ran from May 29th through June 1st. There are three tracks teams get scored on: AutoNav, Self-Drive, and the design challenge.

Project MANAS's fixed-wing programme, pursued in collaboration with the Centre for Development of Advanced Computing (CDAC), is working toward a self-sustainable, high-endurance fixed-wing aircraft. Unlike our multirotor platforms, a fixed-wing airframe can sustain long-duration flight at low power, making it a natural next step for the team as we look beyond short-endurance rotorcraft missions.

Minimizing weight was the central manufacturing challenge, since the aircraft is intended to fly as a glider. We initially explored EPP foam wings with a 3D-printed fuselage, but the airfoil's thin profile needed a comparatively large carbon-fibre spar to resist deflection, so the design shifted to fully 3D-printed wings instead.

Conventional sparse-infill 3D-printed structures came out heavier than targeted, which led us through several wing-design iterations and a material switch from LW-PLA (good density, but a poor surface finish) to PLA+, and finally to PETG after bench testing showed the PLA wing deforming under sunlight and heat.
